Offline and privacy

Your photos are identified on your own computer and are never uploaded. WildID uses the internet to download its parts once and to fetch place lists when you ask for one. It has no account, no telemetry and no update check.

What leaves your computer

When you fetch a place list, WildID sends the name or the id of the place to iNaturalist, and species names from that list to GBIF.

When you fetch lists from the GPS positions of your photos, WildID sends one position for each area of about 28 by 28 km to iNaturalist, to find out which country or region it lies in. The position is one of your photo positions, accurate to about 100 m. If you would rather not send positions, type the country or region by name.

Nothing else is sent. No photo, no file name, no keyword and no usage data leaves your computer, at any time.

Working without a connection

Once the setup is done, identifying photos needs no internet. The setup wizard proves this at its end by running an identification with the network switched off. Before a trip, fetch the place lists for your destinations and WildID will work on a laptop in the field.

The engine on your computer

The engine that does the recognition is a small local server. It listens on your own computer only, on an address other computers on the network cannot reach, and every request must carry a secret key that is stored in your user profile. It stops by itself after 15 minutes without work.

No telemetry

WildID collects no statistics, sends no crash reports and does not check for updates. The libraries it builds on are set up the same way. If a Hugging Face token is saved on your computer, it is used for downloads from huggingface.co and nowhere else.

This website

This site consists of plain pages. It sets no cookies, runs no scripts and loads nothing from other servers. As with any website, the server that hosts it sees the request your browser makes.

WildID

Species identification for wildlife photos, in Lightroom Classic and on the command line. Free and open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence.

Recognition by BioCLIP 2 from the Imageomics Institute. Animal detection by MegaDetector. Species lists from iNaturalist, with names matched against the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy.
